# On/off ramp

The ramp is where privacy and regulation meet. Screening has to happen, but a business does not want every conversion size and frequency exposed onchain the moment funds land.

## What Merces adds

**Screening at the boundary.** AML and sanctions screening runs on funds entering and leaving the private account. The entry and exit points are the right place for compliance — not the inside of private state.

**A clean break on withdrawal.** Deposit and withdrawal amounts are visible, because those are standard token transfers. But nothing links a withdrawal to any specific private transfer: what happened inside the private balance stays inside it.

**No new burden on regulated counterparties.** CASPs and on-ramp providers interacting with the account face no compliance requirements beyond what they already do for public wallets.

**Your compliance stack or ours.** The Compliance Engine is a pluggable slot; Predicate fills it by default, and a compatible provider can be substituted. See [Pre-transaction screening](/docs/finance-solutions/compliance/pre-transaction-screening).

**Disclosure for law enforcement.** Requests are scoped and auditable, returning the selected data and nothing else. See [Post-transaction disclosure](/docs/finance-solutions/compliance/post-transaction-disclosure).
