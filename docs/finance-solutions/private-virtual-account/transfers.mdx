# Transfers

A transfer moves value from one private virtual account to another. Both sides keep their accounts; nothing touches the public token balance.

The sender picks a [privacy mode](docs/finance-solutions/concepts/privacy-model) per transfer. A private transfer encrypts sender, recipient, asset and amount. A confidential transfer encrypts asset and amount, leaving the two wallet addresses visible onchain.

## What a transfer looks like

A sender holding a private balance of 955 USDT selects a recipient and an amount of 42 USDT, signs, and Merces executes. Afterwards the sender's balance is 913 USDT and the recipient's has grown by 42.

Neither balance is public. The recipient learns the amount they received, because they are a party to the transfer; they do not learn the sender's balance. An observer learns neither, and in private mode does not learn who transacted at all.

## How a transfer settles

The client generates the proof locally, then the transfer runs through the same path as any other Merces action:

1. **Proof generation, client-side.** The client splits the amount into three additive secret shares, encrypts each to the corresponding MPC operator's public key, and generates a Groth16 proof that the shares are consistent with the committed amount. Nothing has left the device yet.
2. **Submission.** The client sends proof and ciphertexts to the gateway over WebSocket. The gateway submits the transaction to the Merces contract.
3. **Onchain verification.** The contract verifies the client's proof, records the new commitment, and emits an event that queues the action for the MPC network.
4. **MPC balance update.** The three operators jointly decrypt the ciphertexts, update the sender's and the recipient's secret-shared balances in the same round, and generate a second Groth16 proof that the update was correct. They call `processMPC()` with that proof and the updated commitments.
5. **Finalisation.** The contract verifies the MPC proof and commits the new state. The transaction hash returns through the gateway to the client.

Both proofs are checked by the contract before any commitment moves: the client's proof that it was entitled to spend, and the network's proof that it updated balances correctly.
